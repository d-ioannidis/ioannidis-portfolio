Automation is often described as removing people from a process. In practice, the most durable systems give people a better place in it.

## Automate the repeatable decision

Start with decisions that have clear inputs and a reversible output. Ticket routing is a good example: a system can suggest an owner from workload and expertise, while a coordinator keeps the ability to correct unusual cases.

## Make overrides informative

An override should not be treated as a failure. It is high-quality feedback. Recording a concise reason can reveal missing rules, outdated data, or a new category of work.

## Keep the state visible

People trust automation when they can see what happened, why it happened, and what happens next. A simple activity trail is often more valuable than a more sophisticated model.

The best measure of automation is not how many clicks disappeared. It is whether the process became faster, clearer, and easier to improve.
